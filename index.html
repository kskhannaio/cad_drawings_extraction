Seventeen sheets, one drawing

The elevated-corridor tender (B-SMILE, Schedule A) has 17 vector CAD sheets at 1:2000: topographical survey 1–6, general alignment drawing 1–6 and land acquisition plan 1–5. Each sheet is turned to fit its paper, so no two line up as printed. This is how the pipeline puts them back together and onto the map (step register, steps/s2_register_sheets.py).

The method

  PDF --> L --> M --> R --> C --> ONE
  ONE --> G --> F
  OSM --> F --> OUT
  classDef step fill:#e3f1f3,stroke:#0b6e7a,color:#1d2326
  classDef data fill:#ece9e0,stroke:#8a8576,color:#1d2326
  class L,M,R,C,F step
  class PDF,OSM,G,ONE,OUT data
Each sheet's labels are matched with a neighbouring sheet's, each sheet is fitted to that neighbour (scale, rotation, shift), and the chain of fits brings all 17 into the coordinates of general alignment drawing 2. That one drawing is then fitted to the map so its grey road areas sit on OpenStreetMap road edges.

Survey 2 and land plan 2 link straight to GAD 2; the other sheets reach it through chains of neighbours (for example GAD 6 to GAD 5 to GAD 4 to GAD 3 to land plan 3 to land plan 1 to land plan 2 to GAD 2).

How well it fits

  • Sheet to sheet: shared labels land within 0.1 m of each other after the fit.
  • Same scale everywhere: every sheet comes out at 0.691 m per PDF point (1:2000), to within 0.1%, without being told. That is a good sign the links are right.
  • Drawing to map: on sheets with plenty of road outlines, half the road edges sit within 0.8–1.5 m of OpenStreetMap's, and 71–97% within 3 m.
  • Weak links: survey 6 and GAD 6 share only 2 and 3 labels with their neighbours. Where so few labels are shared, the sheet is placed by a shift only, keeping the common scale and orientation.

Then

With every sheet on the map, the pipeline extracts each line, filled shape and text item, traces the planned structures by fill colour, reads pen-stroke labels with OCR, names the parts from their labels, and exports files for the study website and the QGIS templates. See the repository, and the overview of how the data flows.
